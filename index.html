Admixing of Antibiotics & Antifungals in Bone Cement and Stimulan

Local antimicrobials in PMMA and calcium sulphate carriers for periprosthetic joint infection · Working reference · Draft v2.0 · October 2026

⚠️ Working reference for protocol development — not approved for clinical use. Doses, mixing steps and setting times are drawn from published evidence, project documents and manufacturer/practical charts; several sources disagree and the disagreements are shown rather than hidden. Every regimen must be reviewed by your PJI MDT (orthopaedics and ID/microbiology) and pharmacy/DTC, and checked against the SmPC and local policy. Admixing creates an unlicensed product.

Key positions

A one-screen summary. Grades: A RCT/systematic review · B prospective cohort/registry · C retrospective cohort/case series · D in-vitro/biomechanical/animal · E expert opinion/case report/manufacturer data.

Commercial pre-mixed cement firstUse a manufactured antibiotic-loaded PMMA where it is suitable and available; admix only when the microbiology, allergy or supply situation means a commercial product is not appropriate. Document why. [1,28]
Empirical spacer defaultGentamicin-premixed PMMA with vancomycin hand-admixed (2 g typical; up to 4 g per 40 g for spacers). Hand-admixed vancomycin elutes more than pre-mixed dual cement. Covers ~85–90% of PJI pathogens. [3,4,5,6]
Mechanical ceilingKeep total antibiotic ≤ ~4 g per 40 g (≈10% w/w) when cement is load-bearing (fixation). Strength falls with every gram above ~2 g and over time in vivo; spacers tolerate more but "20% dogma" is not evidence-based. [7,8,9,10]
Best comparative outcome dataVMA (vancomycin 4 g + meropenem 2 g + amphotericin B 200 mg / 40 g): recurrence 1.9% vs 17.7% for GV (p=0.006), retrospective cohort. Grade C. [11]
Never rifampicin in PMMAPrevents polymerisation (soft, friable, discoloured cement). Use calcium sulphate instead if local rifampicin is wanted. [12,13]
Powder, not liquidPrefer sterile powder preparations; liquids alter the powder–liquid ratio and weaken cement. Liquid amikacin is the documented exception, spacers only. [1,14]
Calcium sulphate (Stimulan)Resorbs over weeks, ~10× higher local concentrations, no thermal exposure so heat-labile agents can be used; valuable in DAIR/single-stage. Monitor calcium and renal function. [15,16,17,18]
Stewardship applies locally tooDefine the indication, choose the narrowest effective agent, justify the dose, document the plan. Vancomycin is not a routine addition without microbiological or patient-specific justification. [1]

Decision pathway (working-group structure)

  1. Microbiology result → ID/micro recommendation. Agent chosen against cultures, susceptibility, previous microbiology, allergy, renal function and the systemic plan. [1,28]
  2. MDT decision (orthopaedics + ID/microbiology ± pharmacy). Record indication, microbiological target, cement role (fixation / structural / non-load-bearing spacer), agent, dose per 40 g, number of mixes, monitoring plan, and why commercial cement is unsuitable. [1,28]
  3. Surgical planning. Prescribe and supply pre-operatively; sterile parenteral preparation labelled "for addition to PMMA bone cement only"; written preparation instructions available before the case; patient informed that use is outside product licence. [1,28,29]
  4. Mixing in theatre. Fractionated powder-to-powder mixing before monomer (grind crystalline powders); hand-mix spacers, vacuum-mix fixation cement; document cement, agent, dose per mix, mixes, total dose, method. See Technique. [13,20,28]
  5. Monitoring and audit. Renal function and toxicity monitoring where indicated; mechanical complications recorded; indication, organism, agent, dose, complications and outcome audited. See Safety. [1,29]

Related evidence worth knowing

  • Dose ranges by consensus. The 2013 International Consensus lists per-40 g spacer ranges (vancomycin 0.5–4 g, meropenem 0.5–4 g, daptomycin 2 g, ceftazidime 2 g, cefotaxime 2 g, cefuroxime 1.5–2 g, aztreonam 4 g, voriconazole 300–600 mg); a 2012 review of published spacer regimens found wide variation and no agreed optimum. [2,66]
  • Local therapy and systemic duration. A Sheffield series of 299 two-stage hip revisions using high local antibiotic concentrations in cement beads with a median of five days of systemic antibiotics reported 91% success after reimplantation; the SOLARIO non-inferiority trial (≤7 days vs ≥4 weeks systemic therapy with local antibiotics) will inform this further. [64,65]
  • One-stage vs two-stage. The UK INFORM trial found no superiority of two-stage over single-stage revision for hip PJI at 18 months; single-stage definitive fixation needs preserved cement strength, so admixing must stay conservative. [67]
  • Topical vancomycin powder is a different question. Evidence for loose vancomycin powder in primary arthroplasty is inconclusive and outside the scope of this reference. [68]

Agents

PMMA doses are per 40 g cement, split into fixation (load-bearing) and spacer (non-load-bearing) use where sources distinguish them. Calcium sulphate doses are per 10 cc Stimulan Rapid Cure unless stated. Expand an agent for elution, stability, mixing and linked evidence.

Evidence grade: A RCT/SR B prospective/registry C retrospective/series D in vitro/animal E opinion/case/manufacturer

Carriers

PMMA cements and bioabsorbable carriers used for local delivery in PJI. Brand names are for identification; the working-group standard is brand-neutral. Elution differs between cements (Palacos-type generally elute more than Simplex-type) but structural requirements still govern choice. [1,21]

Combinations

Adding a second agent increases porosity and usually the elution of both ("porogen" effect), at a mechanical cost. Multi-agent, high-dose loading is for spacers, not definitive fixation. [7,9,22]

Theatre technique

PMMA — standard operating steps

  1. Confirm the MDT record, prescription (agent, formulation, dose per 40 g, number of mixes, total dose) and that written preparation instructions are in theatre. [1,28]
  2. Pharmacy-supplied sterile parenteral powder with batch number / certificate; no tablets or capsules (different excipients); no liquid except documented liquid amikacin for spacers. [1,14]
  3. Grind crystalline powders (e.g. vancomycin) to a fine, homogeneous particle size in a sterile bowl; poorly distributed crystals do not elute. [13,28]
  4. Fractionated powder-to-powder mixing: blend the antibiotic with the cement powder in a bowl before adding liquid monomer. Do not dry-homogenise powder inside a cartridge mixing system — it scratches the cartridge and sheds particles. [13,20,28]
  5. Add monomer per manufacturer instructions. Spacers: hand (atmospheric) mix to maximise porosity and elution. Fixation cement: vacuum mix to preserve strength. [1,13,19]
  6. Do not add extra monomer (reduces elution). Optional: the "delayed addition" technique (adding vancomycin ~30 s after mixing starts) increased vancomycin elution by 25–52% in vitro. [23]
  7. Record in the operation note: cement used, antimicrobial(s), dose per mix, number of mixes, total implanted dose, mixing method, and whether the cement was spacer or fixation. [1,29]
Known practice variation: one UK unit's pocket guide mixes cement liquid and powder first and then adds the antibiotic powder, and avoids vacuum mixing for all antibiotic cement [24]. The working-group position is fractionated powder-to-powder mixing before monomer (Kuhn protocol) unless a drug monograph or local protocol states otherwise [13,28]. Laboratory comparison found no mechanical or microbiological difference between careful manual admixing and commercial dual-antibiotic cement, but recommended limiting manual admixing to specific indications and preferring commercial mixtures. [20]

Calcium sulphate (Stimulan Rapid Cure) — general steps

  1. Select standard or Rapid Cure formulation; agent-specific steps and setting times are in each agent card (practical charts and Biocomposites technical letters). [25,26,27]
  2. Powders (vancomycin, teicoplanin, flucloxacillin, fluconazole, voriconazole): mix with Stimulan powder first (~20 s), then add the mixing solution. Some agents set in 2 minutes or less — have the bead mat ready. [25,26]
  3. Agents added to the paste (meropenem, linezolid, ceftazidime, piperacillin-tazobactam, caspofungin): mix Stimulan powder and solution first, then fold in the agent. [25,26,27]
  4. Liquid aminoglycosides (gentamicin / tobramycin 240 mg = 6 mL): use the drug solution instead of the Stimulan mixing solution. [24]
  5. Where saline pre-mixing is specified (amikacin, daptomycin, ertapenem, voriconazole, amphotericin B), respect the stated pre-mix time — "no longer". [25,27]
  6. Deliver as beads or paste into dead space while workable; document as for PMMA. Post-op: serum calcium day 3, day 7, week 3; expect aseptic wound drainage. [16,17,18]
Setting times vary with manufacturer of the drug, dose and theatre temperature; the ability to mix and set does not imply safety or efficacy (manufacturer caveat). Tablets/capsules are not recommended in calcium sulphate (may leave a nidus; IV clindamycin did not set). [25,26,27]

Governance and standards

Good-practice framework for antimicrobial-loaded PMMA (JAC 2026 Viewpoint) [1]

WorkflowGood-practice considerationRationale
Deliberation (pre-op planning)Define indication and microbiological intent before cement selection; apply stewardship principles locally; plan, prescribe and supply pre-operatively.Separates prophylaxis from therapy; local delivery must be justified by drug, dose and method; reduces intra-operative delay and unplanned broad-spectrum admixture.
Drug (selection)Use commercially manufactured antimicrobial-loaded PMMA where suitable; when bespoke therapy is needed select by microbiology, allergy, renal risk, local resistance, licensing and practice; use PMMA-specific guidance; prefer powder over liquid; avoid agents with poor thermal stability, poor elution or adverse effects on polymerisation.Better-characterised handling, dosing, elution and mechanics; liquids impair polymerisation; some agents are inactivated by curing or compromise setting.
Dosing (mechanical safety)Conservative doses when PMMA is used for fixation or structural support.Preserves mechanical properties and reduces systemic toxicity risk.
Delivery (cement and technique)Select cement type for the intended balance of elution vs mechanical demand; standardise and document mixing technique and quantities.Palacos-type cements elute more than Simplex-type; porosity, powder–liquid ratio and technique influence elution and strength.
Delivery (alternative carriers)Consider calcium sulphate, ceramics or other carriers where PMMA is unsuitable for the agent or objective.Different release characteristics, but no fixation function.

Framework intended to support MDT decision-making; not a formal guideline. The same paper notes AKI after high-dose spacers in frail or renally impaired patients, loss of spacer strength over six weeks, and theoretical resistance selection from prolonged sub-inhibitory elution.

Criteria for antimicrobial-loaded cement — regional joint-formulary model adopted by the working group as its starting template [28]

All of the following must be met:

  • Proven or strongly suspected PJI requiring a two-stage procedure with an antibiotic-loaded cement spacer, or antibiotic-loaded cement as fixation cement at the definitive procedure; and
  • the microbiology is such that a commercially available cement is not appropriate; and
  • the wound has been appropriately managed (devitalised tissue and bone debrided or amputated); and
  • the antimicrobial choice has been recommended by ID/microbiology; and
  • the decision, cement and antimicrobial were determined by the orthopaedic MDT with an ID/microbiology consultant present; and
  • PMMA and the antimicrobial are not contraindicated (manufacturer guidance, allergy); and
  • the patient agrees to attend follow-up as defined by the orthopaedic and infection services.

UK regulatory framework (summary) [29]

  • Adding an antimicrobial to a CE/UKCA-marked cement or carrier creates a new, unlicensed product. No manufacturer's instructions permit admixing as a labelled indication; manufacturers provide compatibility data on request.
  • Lawful routes: Section 10 exemption (Medicines Act 1968, preserved under HMR 2012) — preparation under pharmacist supervision to a prescriber's specification for a particular patient, which covers theatre admixing; or a Manufacturer's Specials licence for larger-scale GMP assembly.
  • MHRA Guidance Note 14 (unlicensed "specials"): bona fide special clinical need, prescriber responsibility, documented audit trail, informed consent.
  • A DTC-approved admixing formulary pre-authorises named regimens, standardises consent and documentation, and removes per-case unlicensed-medicine forms; agents outside the list (antifungals, daptomycin, etc.) still need individual authorisation.
  • Supply: antimicrobial vials via hospital pharmacy under parenteral-stock controls, not via device channels; theatre top-up stock logged; Yellow Card reporting for adverse events.
  • Related frameworks: NICE NG125 (antibiotic-loaded cement for prophylaxis in cemented arthroplasty); BOA/BASK/BHS PJI best practice (spacers in two-stage revision); NHS England commissioning policy mandating MDT for revision PJI; SPS unlicensed-medicines guidance.

Working-group decisions (first meeting, June 2026) [28]

  • Two-tier output: a short governance guideline structured around the decision pathway, plus RNOH-led drug monographs (systematic review + modified Delphi; meropenem nearly complete, daptomycin and an antifungal next).
  • Scope PMMA only for now; cement kept generic; commercial pre-mixed cement recommended where available (also covers supply shortages); start with ~4–5 antimicrobials plus one antifungal.
  • Practical "how to mix" guidance and an explicit "do not add" list (rifampicin) to be included; professional endorsement to be sought so that DTCs recognise the guidance; review roughly every two years.

About this reference

This single-file web reference consolidates the working evidence base on adding antibiotics and antifungals to PMMA bone cement and calcium sulphate carriers (Stimulan, Cerament) for periprosthetic joint infection. It is intended as a shareable quick reference for UK PJI teams (including UHCW) while formal national guidance and drug monographs are developed.

How to use: browse or search Agents and expand any card for doses (fixation vs spacer), elution, stability, mixing steps and linked evidence. Carriers and Combinations summarise cement/carrier choice and multi-agent regimens. Technique, Governance and Safety carry the process content from the JAC 2026 framework, the regional formulary criteria and the project SOP.

Sources: the graded project evidence review; the Antimicrobial Mixing Guide 2025; the June 2026 Evidence Synthesis; working-group agenda and minutes (June 2026); Azamgarhi et al., JAC 2026; Sheffield pocket guide (Sharrock, Babu, Petrie, v1.0 Jan 2026); Stimulan practical mixing charts and Biocomposites technical letters (2016, 2019, 2023); North Bristol antifungal assay information; and the primary papers listed under References.
